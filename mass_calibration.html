Mass calibration

A time-of-flight instrument measures time, not mass. Converting time to m/z uses a calibration fitted to ions of known mass, and that calibration drifts. Here the instrument has drifted since it was last calibrated; refit it and see where the error ends up.

Errors are RMS over m/z 100–2,200. Flight time follows t = t₀ + k√m, so a TOF calibration is naturally a fit in √m; real instruments add small extra terms.

Try this

  1. With the 2-parameter model and all 8 calibrants, the drift and low-mass error vanish, but a small curve remains: the model has no term for the high-mass error. Switch to 3 parameters.
  2. Choose “Low mass only.” The fit is excellent below m/z 700 and increasingly wrong above it. Calibrations interpolate well and extrapolate badly.
  3. Turn on the mismatched calibrant. Least squares bends the whole curve toward the bad point. Turn on the robust fit and it is set aside.
  4. Pick the 3-parameter model with two calibrants. There isn’t enough information to fit it.

Key ideas

In a TOF, ions accelerated through the same voltage fly with speed proportional to 1/√(m/z), so flight time grows with √(m/z). Calibration fits that relationship to ions of known mass; more terms model more of the instrument’s quirks but need more calibrants.

Calibrants should span the whole m/z range you care about. Errors grow quickly outside the calibrated range.

During long runs, instruments drift with temperature and electronics. Lock masses, ions present in every scan, let software refit or correct the calibration as it goes. Robust fitting protects against the occasional wrong match.

Where these ideas come from

The original papers and books behind the concepts in this lesson.

  • Time-of-flight and why flight time grows with √(m/z)

    Wiley, W. C. & McLaren, I. H. (1955). Time-of-flight mass spectrometer with improved resolution. Review of Scientific Instruments 26(12), 1150–1157.

  • Internal calibration with known ions

    Palmblad, M., Bindschedler, L. V., Gibson, T. M. & Cramer, R. (2006). Automatic internal calibration in liquid chromatography/Fourier transform ion cyclotron resonance mass spectrometry of protein digests. Rapid Communications in Mass Spectrometry 20(20), 3076–3080.

  • Lock masses for continuous recalibration

    Olsen, J. V., de Godoy, L. M. F., Li, G., Macek, B., Mortensen, P., Pesch, R., Makarov, A., Lange, O., Horning, S. & Mann, M. (2005). Parts per million mass accuracy on an Orbitrap mass spectrometer via lock mass injection into a C-trap. Molecular & Cellular Proteomics 4(12), 2010–2021.

  • Instrument-specific calibration functions

    Marissen, R. & Palmblad, M. (2021). mzRecal: universal MS1 recalibration in mzML using identified peptides in mzIdentML as internal calibrants. Bioinformatics, btab056.

  • Robust fitting that rejects outliers

    Huber, P. J. (1964). Robust estimation of a location parameter. Annals of Mathematical Statistics 35(1), 73–101.

    Fischler, M. A. & Bolles, R. C. (1981). Random sample consensus: a paradigm for model fitting with applications to image analysis and automated cartography. Communications of the ACM 24(6), 381–395.

  • Recalibration pixel by pixel in imaging

    La Rocca, R., Kune, C., Tiquet, M., Stuart, L., Eppe, G., Alexandrov, T., De Pauw, E. & Quinton, L. (2021). Adaptive pixel mass recalibration for mass spectrometry imaging based on locally endogenous biological signals. Analytical Chemistry 93(8), 4066–4074.
