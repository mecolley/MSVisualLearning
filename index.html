Mass spectrometry signal processing, one idea at a time

Interactive lessons on what happens between the detector and a peak list: what a peak is, what noise is, and how software decides where a peak is and how much is there. Each lesson runs entirely in your browser.

Lessons

Listed in a suggested order, from the raw signal to a calibrated peak list.

  1. Anatomy of a spectrum

    How a stream of ion arrivals becomes a sampled profile, and why every peak at a given m/z has a predictable width.

    Covers: sampling, resolving power and FWHM, profile versus centroid data

    Open lesson

  2. What noise looks like

    Counting, electronic and chemical noise side by side, and why only some of them average away.

    Covers: Poisson statistics, detector noise, chemical background, baseline

    Open lesson

  3. Smoothing and baseline removal

    Noise is narrower than a peak and baseline is wider, but a crowd of unresolved peaks can look like baseline too. See how filters use these differences on a low-resolution TOF spectrum, and what they cost.

    Covers: moving average, Savitzky–Golay, rolling-minimum and SNIP baselines, unresolved peaks

    Open lesson

  4. Detecting peaks

    Set a signal-to-noise threshold and count the false peaks it lets through in a long spectrum.

    Covers: noise estimation, thresholds, multiple testing, wavelet detection

    Open lesson

  5. Finding a peak’s center

    Five ways to estimate a peak’s m/z from sampled points, compared under different sampling and noise.

    Covers: apex, center of mass, parabolic and Gaussian interpolation, least-squares fitting, the counting limit

    Open lesson

  6. Overlapping peaks and resolution

    Slide two peaks together and watch them merge into one, and what that does to the reported m/z.

    Covers: resolving power, unresolved pairs, peak width as a diagnostic

    Open lesson

  7. Mass calibration

    Turn flight times into m/z, then correct the drift using ions of known mass.

    Covers: TOF calibration functions, lock masses, ppm error, robust fitting

    Open lesson

  8. Combining many scans

    Sum scans first or pick peaks first? Compare both, with and without drift between scans.

    Covers: signal averaging, square-root-of-N gains, peak matching across scans

    Open lesson

The lessons use simulated data, so the true answer is always known. Nothing you do on these pages is sent anywhere.

Each lesson ends with references to the papers and books where its ideas originated.
